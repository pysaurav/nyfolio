I am a backend engineer who moved into AI. Based in Oslo, Norway.

## What I do

Currently at Izy I work on both halves of the stack. The production backend
serving Norwegian commercial buildings, and the AI running on it. Computer vision
self-checkout for workplace canteens, and demand forecasting so kitchens buy the
right amount of food. Both in production, both in daily use.

Keeping a heavy system running is a different discipline from building a model.
Doing both is most of what I have to offer.

Eight years across backend architecture, machine learning and technical product,
from startups to enterprise. Distributed services, data pipelines, ETL, and
systems that had to stay up as they grew. Most of it before the current AI cycle
began.

## Why ethics is not a side interest

The gap between a model that works and a system you can defend is where most AI
projects quietly fail. I spent 160 hours studying AI ethics and fairness formally
through Turing College. Fairness metrics, causal interventions, governance,
production deployment.

One idea from it changed how I think. The impossibility theorem says you cannot
satisfy every reasonable definition of fairness at once. So "is this system
fair?" has no purely technical answer. Somebody has to choose, and they should
choose deliberately. I wrote about that in
[Coding Is Solved. The Hard Part Starts Now.](/posts/coding-is-solved-the-hard-part-starts-now/)
