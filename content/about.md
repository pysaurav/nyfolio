I build AI that runs in production, and I write about who carries the risk when
it fails. Based in Oslo, Norway.

## The builder

I am a Technical Product Manager at Izy, a Norwegian workplace platform running
in more than 200 commercial buildings across Norway. I lead both halves of the
stack: the production backend, and the AI running on top of it.

- **The platform.** I maintain the backend behind the whole platform, around 750
  API endpoints serving apps, admin tools and partners, and hundreds of thousands
  of transactions a month.
- **The AI.** Computer vision self-checkout for workplace canteens, and demand
  forecasting so kitchens buy the right amount of food. Both in production, both
  in daily use.
- **The integrations.** I own the integrations between Izy and third-party
  companies, from payments to building systems, where most of the real risk in a
  platform lives.
- **The initiatives.** I start things, not only maintain them: bringing AI into
  how we build software, and turning ideas into tools that run in production.

I did not come to AI with the hype. My final-year project in 2017 was machine
learning, built with scikit-learn, and became my first published paper on
anomaly-based intrusion detection. Since then, eight years across backend
architecture, machine learning and technical product, from startups to
enterprise. Distributed services, data
pipelines and systems that had to stay up as they grew. Most of it before the
current AI cycle began.

Keeping a heavy system running is a different discipline from building a model.
Doing both is most of what I have to offer.

## The writer

When building gets cheap, judgement and accountability become the scarce part.
That is what I write about.

My working rule is **[the sleep test](/sleep-test/)**. At every point of no return, whether money,
personal data or anything a customer sees, ask one question: *who loses sleep if
this goes wrong?* If the answer is a team or a process and not a name, you are
not ready. It was first published in Norwegian in
[Dagens Perspektiv](https://www.dagensperspektiv.no/synspunkt/ki-mister-ikke-nattesovnen-det-er-problemet/1465374)
in November 2026, and in English
[here](/posts/ai-can-calculate-a-risk-it-cant-carry-one/).

The ethics side is studied, not borrowed. I spent 160 hours on AI ethics and
fairness through Turing College: fairness metrics, causal interventions,
governance, production deployment. I have also completed AWS training in
responsible AI. The idea that stayed with me is the
impossibility theorem. No system can satisfy every reasonable definition of
fairness at once, so somebody has to choose, and they should choose deliberately.
More in
[Coding Is Solved. The Hard Part Starts Now.](/posts/coding-is-solved-the-hard-part-starts-now/)
